Polynomial structure for deployable proprioceptive control

PRISM: Polynomial Representations for Interaction-Structured Motor Control

Seung Hyun Lee · Stella X. Yu

Computer Science and Engineering · University of Michigan, Ann Arbor

Robot behavior depends on relationships among configuration, velocity, commands, and recent actions. PRISM gives policies a compact polynomial basis for learning those relationships without changing their sensors or control interface.

Read the paper Explore results View code

01 / Motivation

Interactions add context.

Each coordinate remains informative on its own. PRISM also lets the policy model how signals jointly shape behavior.

configuration×velocity

can encode how motion effects vary with body pose

command×response

can distinguish similar motion under different goals

recent action×state change

can summarize how the body responded over time

02 / Method

PRISM learns polynomial state interactions.

Learned affine factors and their products form a low-degree state representation. The factorization avoids enumerating every coordinate pair and is trained jointly with the policy.

Deployable input Proprioceptive history x
jointsvelocitiesactionscommands

PRISM Factorized polynomial representation
first-order path factor × factor higher degree
The affine factors and their products are learned end to end.
→
Policy input Conditioned state consumed by the same downstream policy
Compact factorization avoids an explicit all-pairs expansion. No extra sensing or privileged dynamics labels are added. Interface-preserving integration changes only state conditioning.

05 / Mechanism analysis

What changes inside the representation?

The arXiv paper uses frozen-feature probes and factor ablations. A BFM-Zero t-SNE then provides a complementary strong-backbone view.

Frozen linear probes

Do physical-response proxies become easier to decode?

Slip, joint power, contact impulse, and contact work are computed only for analysis from held-out rollouts.

Latent factor ablation

Which learned factors most affect the action?

Dominant weights reveal velocity-memory, cross-joint, and state-velocity structure; these terms are not hand-coded.

Paper evidence These diagnostics support representation-level interpretation, not causal identification of unobserved physics.

Takeaway

Richer state. Same interface.

Controlled MLP and Diffusion Policy experiments isolate the representation effect; BFM-Zero and SmolVLA show that it persists on stronger backbones. Every comparison keeps deployment sensing and action interfaces fixed.

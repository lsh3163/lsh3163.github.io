Polynomial structure for deployable proprioceptive control

PRISM: Polynomial Representations for Interaction-Structured Motor Control

Seung Hyun Lee · Stella X. Yu

Computer Science and Engineering · University of Michigan, Ann Arbor

Policies observe positions, velocities, commands, and action histories as separate coordinates. Yet control-relevant mechanics is often multiplicative: power couples effort with velocity, inertial effects couple velocities, and contact or slip leaves coupled signatures over time. PRISM exposes a compact, learnable polynomial basis for these interactions without adding sensors.

01 / Motivation

Why add a polynomial basis?

A policy receives first-order coordinates, while many mechanics-relevant cues have multiplicative structure. Standard affine projections must synthesize those products indirectly; PRISM makes learned low-degree products available while retaining the original signals.

Takeaway

Richer state. Same interface.
